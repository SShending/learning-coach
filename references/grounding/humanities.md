# History And Humanities Source Selection

Read this when grounding historical, textual, cultural, or humanities claims whose source status or interpretation matters.

Distinguish between:

- what a primary or near-contemporary source records;
- what later historical sources record;
- what modern scholarship infers or argues;
- what remains contested or uncertain.

Primary sources are evidence, not automatic truth. Consider authorship, proximity, purpose, bias, genre, survival, translation, and corroboration when those factors materially affect the claim.

Do not turn one source's account into an unqualified fact when the learning task depends on source criticism or competing interpretations.
