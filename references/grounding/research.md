# Research And Science Source Selection

Read this when grounding papers, scientific results, datasets, empirical findings, or claims about a research field.

Prefer, as applicable:

1. the original paper for the authors' actual claims, methods, and reported results;
2. primary datasets/code when implementation or empirical details matter;
3. subsequent papers, replications, systematic reviews, or authoritative surveys when evaluating whether a result generalizes or still holds;
4. later critiques or corrections when interpretation is disputed.

Do not teach an original paper's conclusion as settled field-wide consensus merely because the paper reported it. Separate:

- what the paper itself claims;
- what its experiment/data support;
- what later evidence supports;
- what remains uncertain or contested.

When teaching a rapidly evolving research frontier, refresh sources before using a claim as an assessment key or durable note.
